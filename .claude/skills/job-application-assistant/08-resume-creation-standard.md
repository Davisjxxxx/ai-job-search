# Evidence-Grounded Resume Creation Standard

**Version:** 2.0  
**Updated:** October 2026  
**Status:** Governing standard for resume creation and tailoring in the Job Radar workflow

## 1. Purpose

This standard governs how resumes are created, reviewed, tailored, and validated. It combines an evidence-based resume design framework with the strongest controls from Generation's AI Resume Reviewer workflow.

The objective is not to "beat an ATS." The objective is to produce a resume that:

1. is factually defensible;
2. states genuinely supported qualifications plainly;
3. is easy for a recruiter to scan;
4. is tailored to the actual vacancy without keyword stuffing;
5. preserves the candidate's strongest evidence rather than compressing it into generic language; and
6. prepares the candidate to defend every material claim in an interview.

When this standard conflicts with older resume-template guidance, this standard takes precedence.

---

## 2. Governing Principles

### 2.1 Evidence before wording

No wording improvement can justify an unsupported claim. Every material claim must be grounded in candidate source material.

Material claims include:
- achievements;
- metrics;
- scope statements;
- credentials;
- skills;
- tools and platforms;
- leadership claims;
- ownership claims;
- regulatory or domain expertise; and
- dates, titles, employers, and locations.

The system must never invent an achievement, metric, credential, skill, or level of ownership.

### 2.2 Foundational resume before vacancy tailoring

Use a two-stage process:

1. **Foundational resume:** establish a strong, accurate, readable base document.
2. **Vacancy-tailored resume:** align the base document to one specific role.

Do not use vacancy keywords to repair a weak foundational resume. Tailoring is a final alignment pass, not a substitute for evidence or structure.

### 2.3 Human approval remains final

AI suggestions are recommendations, not authoritative edits. The candidate must retain the ability to accept, revise, regenerate, or reject each substantive change.

### 2.4 Optimize for human scanning and retrieval

The resume must work for two audiences:
- a recruiter or hiring manager scanning quickly; and
- a recruiting system parsing fields and supporting recruiter search or ranking.

Formatting should reduce parsing risk, but content quality and explicit qualification matching take priority over ATS folklore.

### 2.5 Tailor by evidence, not by keyword density

Use the employer's terminology when it accurately describes the candidate's experience. Do not add terms only because they appear in the posting.

## 3. Evidence Hierarchy and Claim Status

Before drafting, build or refresh a source-of-truth inventory from available candidate materials, such as:

- master resume/CV;
- prior verified resumes;
- LinkedIn export;
- performance reviews;
- project notes;
- certificates and diplomas;
- portfolio or GitHub evidence;
- reference letters;
- prior application materials; and
- candidate-confirmed facts.

Each material claim should be assigned one of these statuses:

| Status | Meaning | Resume use |
|---|---|---|
| **VERIFIED_DIRECT** | Explicitly supported by source evidence | May be stated directly |
| **VERIFIED_EQUIVALENT** | Source evidence supports the capability, but wording differs from the vacancy | May be reframed accurately |
| **PARTIAL** | Candidate has adjacent or limited exposure | State narrowly; do not imply full proficiency |
| **UNVERIFIED** | Plausible but not yet supported | Do not publish until confirmed |
| **UNSUPPORTED_GAP** | Requirement is not supported by available evidence | Do not claim; identify as a gap |

If evidence is ambiguous, downgrade the claim rather than strengthening the language.

## 4. Vacancy Requirement Map

Before tailoring, decompose the job posting into a requirement map.

Classify each item as:
- required qualification;
- preferred qualification;
- core responsibility;
- domain knowledge;
- tool or platform;
- leadership/scope expectation;
- eligibility or knockout criterion; or
- cultural/behavioral signal.

Then map each requirement to candidate evidence:

| Vacancy requirement | Importance | Candidate status | Evidence source | Resume action |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Example requirement | Required | VERIFIED_DIRECT | Role/project evidence | State plainly in experience |
| Example tool | Preferred | PARTIAL | Limited project exposure | Use narrow wording |
| Example credential | Required | UNSUPPORTED_GAP | None | Do not claim; flag gap |

This map is the basis for tailoring. It prevents both omission of real qualifications and fabrication of missing ones.

## 5. Resume Design Rules

### 5.1 Page length

Page count is not an ATS rule.

Use the shortest document that preserves relevant evidence and remains easy to scan.

- One page can be appropriate for early-career candidates or genuinely narrow histories.
- Two pages are defensible and often preferable for experienced, mid-level, and managerial candidates when the second page contains relevant evidence.
- Do not force a resume to one page by deleting quantified outcomes or role-defining evidence.
- Do not force a resume to two pages by adding filler.

For an experienced candidate, two pages should normally be treated as a target, not as a hard technical requirement.

### 5.2 Layout

Use simple, ATS-safe structure:

- single-column layout;
- standard section headings;
- no layout tables;
- no text boxes or floating shapes;
- no skill bars, rating dots, charts, or icons that carry essential information;
- no critical content in headers or footers;
- contact information in the document body;
- no full street address unless specifically required;
- consistent conventional date formats;
- standard bullets; and
- text-based PDF or DOCX output.

Bold, italics, underlining, standard bullets, and a normal skills section are acceptable.

### 5.3 Typography and spacing

Treat exact font and margin rules as conventions, not scientific laws.

Practical defaults:
- standard system font;
- 10-12 pt body text;
- 0.5-1.0 inch margins;
- single to 1.15 line spacing;
- visible separation between roles and sections; and
- no content compression below readable limits merely to hit a page target.

### 5.4 Bullet length and information density

Control readability through bullet length, not narrow columns.

- One line is ideal when the claim remains complete.
- Two lines are normal and acceptable.
- Three or more lines usually indicate a paragraph disguised as a bullet and should be split or cut.
- Front-load the result, scope, or differentiating fact in the first few words whenever possible.

### 5.5 Relevance-weighted bullet count

Allocate space by relevance:
- most recent and most relevant roles: approximately 4-6 bullets;
- prior relevant roles: approximately 2-4 bullets;
- older or less relevant roles: approximately 0-2 bullets or a compressed earlier-experience line.

These are guides, not quotas. An older achievement that directly proves a critical requirement can outrank a recent low-value duty statement.

### 5.6 What to cut first

Cut in this order:

1. redundancy;
2. duty statements with no differentiating value;
3. generic profile language;
4. low-relevance bullets;
5. low-relevance supporting content;
6. duplicated skills already proven in experience, unless the term is useful for recruiter search; and
7. low-relevance older material.

Do not cut quantified outcomes, dates, employer names, or job titles merely to save space.

### 5.7 References

Do not include references or "references available upon request" on the resume unless the employer or local convention specifically requires them. Use the space for evidence that affects selection.

## 6. Content Rules by Section

### 6.1 Header

Include only useful contact and professional-link information:
- name;
- city/state or relevant location;
- phone;
- email;
- LinkedIn; and
- portfolio/GitHub when relevant.

Do not require a full street address.

### 6.2 Professional summary

Use a short role-specific summary only when it adds information.

Target approximately 2-4 lines. It should state the candidate's strongest relevant positioning, domain depth, and differentiating value. Do not repeat the skills section or fill space with traits that are not evidenced.

### 6.3 Skills

Use plain text categories and searchable terminology. Prioritize:
- terms explicitly relevant to the vacancy;
- genuinely supported tools and methods; and
- domain capabilities that may not be obvious from job titles.

Do not use proficiency graphics or self-rating bars.

### 6.4 Professional experience

Prioritize evidence over duties.

Strong bullets normally contain one or more of:
- result;
- scale;
- decision authority;
- risk controlled;
- process changed;
- stakeholders influenced;
- quality improved;
- time or cost reduced;
- volume handled; or
- complex problem solved.

Use metrics only when they are supported. Never generate a number because a bullet "would be stronger" with one.

### 6.5 Education and certifications

Include credentials that support role fit or career credibility. Keep older or less relevant entries concise.

### 6.6 Projects

Projects may be used when they demonstrate a relevant capability not adequately represented in employment history. Distinguish clearly between:
- production work;
- personal projects;
- coursework;
- prototypes;
- experiments; and
- work in progress.

Do not present experimental or learning work as production ownership.

## 7. Tailoring Protocol

### Step 1 - Build the foundational resume

Create the strongest accurate base document before applying role-specific changes.

Check:
- factual accuracy;
- section structure;
- relevance-weighted content;
- readable bullet length;
- ATS-safe layout; and
- removal of outdated filler.

### Step 2 - Parse the vacancy

Create the requirement map described in Section 4.

### Step 3 - Run a fit and gap analysis

For each high-value requirement, assign one of the evidence statuses from Section 3.

Do not hide genuine gaps. Distinguish:
- direct match;
- equivalent transferable match;
- partial exposure; and
- unsupported gap.

### Step 4 - Select evidence before rewriting

Choose the strongest factual examples that prove the highest-value requirements. Tailoring may require selecting a different achievement, not merely rewriting the existing bullet.

### Step 5 - Rewrite using employer terminology where accurate

Use the vacancy's language when it is a truthful description of the candidate's work.

Prefer concrete evidence in experience bullets over abstract claims in the summary.

### Step 6 - Section-by-section review

Review:
- Header;
- Summary;
- Skills;
- Professional Experience;
- Education;
- Certifications;
- Projects, if used;
- Formatting; and
- Claim Grounding.

For substantive changes, the reviewer should explain why the change improves fit, evidence, readability, or accuracy.

### Step 7 - Claim-grounding audit

Before finalization, verify every material claim against source evidence.

The final document must contain no **UNVERIFIED** claims.

### Step 8 - Final vacancy-alignment pass

Confirm that genuinely supported named qualifications are stated plainly. Do not rely on implied equivalence when the posting uses a specific term and the candidate legitimately has that qualification.

Do not keyword-stuff. Repeated terminology without evidence is a negative signal, not an optimization.

### Step 9 - Visual and parsing-risk QA

Check:
- page breaks;
- orphaned headings or role titles;
- clipping;
- dense paragraphs;
- inconsistent dates;
- unusual symbols;
- contact information location;
- single-column reading order; and
- text extraction from the final PDF.

### Step 10 - Interview-defense output

Generate a separate interview-preparation ledger from the final resume.

For each high-salience claim, include:
- the exact resume claim;
- source/evidence behind it;
- likely interviewer question;
- concise proof point or STAR example; and
- any boundary the candidate should not overstate.

Every number and strong claim on the resume should be something the candidate can explain without improvising facts.

## 8. Reviewer Behavior

The reviewer should not merely score the resume. It should expose the reasoning behind recommendations.

For each proposed substantive edit, provide:
- current wording;
- suggested wording;
- reason;
- requirement addressed, if applicable;
- evidence status; and
- source reference or evidence note.

The candidate may accept, modify, regenerate, or reject the suggestion.

### Quick wins

Low-risk mechanical changes may be grouped as quick wins, including:
- inconsistent dates;
- obvious spelling/grammar issues;
- whitespace cleanup;
- conventional section labels; and
- minor wording fixes that do not alter meaning.

Changes that alter meaning, scope, ownership, proficiency, or metrics must never be bulk-approved without explicit review.

## 9. Quality Gates

A resume is ready only when all gates pass.

### Gate A - Factual integrity
- No fabricated skills, achievements, metrics, credentials, or ownership.
- All dates, titles, employers, and contact details verified.
- No UNVERIFIED claim remains.

### Gate B - Vacancy alignment
- Required qualifications are either explicitly supported or explicitly recognized as gaps.
- High-value direct matches appear in prominent sections.
- Employer terminology is used only when accurate.

### Gate C - Human readability
- Strong first page.
- Clear standard headings.
- Most bullets are one or two lines.
- Outcomes and differentiators are front-loaded.
- No obvious filler.

### Gate D - Parsing-risk reduction
- Single-column reading order.
- No essential information in headers/footers, graphics, text boxes, or layout tables.
- Standard dates and headings.
- Final PDF contains selectable/extractable text.

### Gate E - Interview defensibility
- Every high-salience claim has a defensible source or example.
- Metrics and ownership claims have clear boundaries.
- Interview-prep ledger generated.

---

## 10. Prohibited Practices

Do not:
- fabricate missing qualifications;
- infer proficiency beyond evidence;
- generate unsupported metrics;
- use white-text keywords;
- stuff keywords without context;
- hide gaps through ambiguous language;
- imply ownership when the candidate was a contributor or SME;
- use a one-page limit as justification to remove differentiating evidence;
- compress fonts or margins below readable levels to force page count; or
- treat an AI score as equivalent to an employer's hiring decision.

---

## 11. Recommended Output Package

For each serious application, produce:

1. **Foundational resume** - strong general version.
2. **Tailored resume** - role-specific version.
3. **Requirement map** - posting-to-evidence analysis.
4. **Claim-grounding ledger** - support status for material claims.
5. **Gap list** - real requirements that are not supported.
6. **Interview-defense sheet** - proof points for the final resume.
7. **Final QA checklist** - factual, alignment, readability, parsing-risk, and visual checks.

The resume itself stays concise. The evidence and reasoning live in supporting artifacts.

## 12. Evidence Basis and Caveats

This standard combines two inputs:

1. **Resume Design: What the Evidence Actually Supports** (September 2026), which reviews evidence and conventions related to page length, readability, recruiter scanning, ATS behavior, and parsing risk.
2. **Generation AI Resume Reviewer v1 - Learner Portal User Guide** (September 2026), especially its foundational-vs-tailored workflow, section-by-section review, grounded-content controls, candidate approval model, and interview-preparation output.

Important evidence caveats from the resume research remain in force:
- page-length evidence includes a vendor-run hiring simulation and should not be treated as universal law;
- ATS behavior varies by employer configuration;
- much formatting advice is practitioner consensus rather than controlled research;
- some employer-filter evidence is several years old; and
- typography research about sustained reading is not identical to resume scanning.

Therefore the framework uses evidence quality explicitly and avoids presenting craft conventions as settled science.

---

## 13. Design Philosophy

The system should optimize for **truthful signal transmission**, not cosmetic scoring.

A strong AI resume reviewer should answer five questions:

1. **What does the candidate actually have evidence for?**
2. **What does this employer actually ask for?**
3. **Where is the strongest evidence for each important requirement?**
4. **How can that evidence be stated clearly without exaggeration?**
5. **Can the candidate defend every important statement in an interview?**

If those five questions are answered well, ATS compatibility becomes a hygiene requirement rather than the center of the product.
